# Digi's G12 visual target

The objective is a convincing G12-like photograph from iPhone capture using internet references. Paired physical G12 access is not a prerequisite. Camera-default output, named camera presets and edited social-media aesthetics remain separate reference classes. This prevents an unrelated editing trend from silently redefining the camera target.

## Characteristics to target

| Characteristic | Evidence | What Digi should test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Selective rich color | Imaging Resource's G12 tests describe stronger reds/greens/browns/blues, with different behavior in yellow/cyan and hue shifts. | Adjust hue regions separately. Preserve skin and neutral objects. Do not apply a universal saturation multiplier. |
| Lighting-dependent white balance | Review results and owner experiences vary with illuminant, WB setting and edits. | Test daylight, shade, warm indoor and mixed light separately. A permanent yellow cast is not the G12's complete signature. |
| Clear low-ISO detail | DPReview finds high quality through ISO 400; noise and noise reduction are more visible at higher sensitivities. | Keep daylight relatively clean. Avoid a fixed heavy grain overlay. |
| Higher-ISO texture and reduced fine detail/color | PhotographyBlog reports increasing noise and desaturation as ISO rises. | Eventually model noise and detail separately, using full-resolution samples. G12 and iPhone ISO numbers are not interchangeable noise measurements. |
| Strong direct-flash aesthetic | Firsthand owners praise flash portraits and sunny/backlit use; some disclose editing. | Test real flash plus ambient exposure. A LUT alone cannot reproduce flash power, physical shadow placement or distance falloff. |
| Multiple in-camera looks | Canon's manual distinguishes My Colors Off, Vivid, Neutral, Positive Film and custom controls. | Keep default and Positive Film targets separate. Positive Film is a color preset, not proof of actual film grain. |
| 10-megapixel compact-camera detail and lens behavior | G12 specifications list 3648×2736 output and a 28–140mm-equivalent zoom; PhotographyBlog notes some wide-angle corner softness. | Assess image-size/detail response and framing separately from color. Do not create blanket blur or shallow portrait-mode bokeh as a shortcut. |
| Scene/exposure-specific contrast | The camera offers exposure control and i-Contrast/HDR-related choices; original samples show varying highlights and shadows. | Preserve highlight structure and deliberate dark backgrounds. Do not assume every G12 photo requires crushed blacks, faded blacks or clipped windows. |

Sources: [Imaging Resource's camera tests](https://www.imaging-resource.com/cameras/canon-g12-review/), [DPReview's G12 review and specifications](https://www.dpreview.com/reviews/4522799364/canong12/), [PhotographyBlog's own tests](https://www.photographyblog.com/reviews/canon_powershot_g12_review), [Canon's user guide, page 88](https://gdlp01.c-wss.com/gds/1/0300004191/01/PSG12_CUG_EN.pdf), and [firsthand owner experience](https://weibo.com/6631077872/PpoJsC2tK). The linked [owner-research report](2026-09-15-g12-owner-style-research.md) records confidence and editing caveats.

## What is not established

There is no universal numeric G12 warmth, hue shift, saturation or grain setting. CCD technology alone does not specify the final JPEG aesthetic. Lens, white balance, exposure, camera processing and editing all matter. The model cannot reconstruct spectral sensor information or lighting that was never captured. These limitations guide validation; they do not prevent building a useful aesthetic approximation.

## Acceptance checklist

Use a consistent comparison set: daylight foliage/sky, skin across several complexions, white/gray objects, saturated fabric, warm indoor, colored LED/mixed light, backlit windows and night flash. Compare original, installed profile and candidate outputs at equal display size and in detail crops. Reject green/magenta skin shifts, hue discontinuities, muddy shadows, banding, hard clipping, excessive grain and preview flicker.

Rank candidate resemblance and user preference separately from numerical distribution similarity. Internet photos are unrelated scenes, so no percentage or pixel-error metric measures exact camera accuracy. The current model covers color/tone; flash, noise, optics and lighting adaptation require their own experiments.
